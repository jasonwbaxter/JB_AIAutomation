The 10 switches that start a safe Microsoft 365 Copilot deployment

Measure first. Then turn on these ten settings across Copilot, agents, MCPs and connectors. One admin, two to three days of focused work, before a single user sees Copilot.

Audience: AI, Copilot, SharePoint, Teams and Security admins Pilot size: 50–200 users across 3+ functions Time: 2–3 days before pilot, then ongoing

Before you touch a setting: measure

Three organisations with the same Copilot licence end up in three different places because they start from different baselines. Spend one day with the reports below so you’re deploying on evidence, not assumption.

  • Microsoft 365 usage reports (admin.microsoft.com → Reports → Usage): active users per app, 7 to 180 days. Shows which apps your people already live in. Copilot gives the fastest wins there.
  • Microsoft Adoption Score (Reports → Adoption Score): six categories now total 600 points, including AI adoption. Benchmarks against peer organisations. Weak categories tell you where your training and change plan should focus.
  • Microsoft Secure Score (Microsoft Defender portal): your security baseline before you widen access with Copilot. Fix the top five recommended actions first.
  • SharePoint Advanced Management content assessment (SharePoint admin center → Reports): sites with oversized audiences, Everyone-except-external-users, ownerless and inactive. This is the list that drives switch 5.
  • Microsoft Purview DSPM data risk assessment (Purview portal): overshared sites with sensitive data, risky sharing links, high-interaction content. This is the list that drives switch 6.
  • Collaboration patterns: in the admin center usage reports and Adoption Score, look at Teams messages, meeting recordings, SharePoint sites visited and OneDrive files shared. Chat-heavy orgs get fast value from Teams recap; document-heavy orgs get fast value from Word, Excel and SharePoint agents.

Write down three numbers you want to move in 90 days (e.g. meeting-recap usage, documents chatted-with, oversharing-flagged sites remediated). You’ll need them for switch 10’s cost review.

Switch 1 · Copilot

Assign licences to a focused pilot, not “everyone with E5”

30 min
Old way
Light up Copilot for every eligible user on day one.
→
→
New way
Assign to a 50–200 person pilot across three or more functions, with champions, before a tenant-wide rollout.

Why it matters

A broad switch-on hides your early failures and burns the first impression. A focused pilot gives you measurable usage per team, honest feedback and the pattern your training plan builds on.

Where to turn it on · 10 min

Microsoft 365 admin center → Billing → Licenses. Assign Microsoft 365 Copilot to a security group that holds your pilot users. Add the AI Administrator role to one named owner.

✓ Done when

  • Pilot group has owners, champions and a Teams channel
  • Three success measures agreed with the sponsor

Switch 2 · Copilot

Turn on the apps Copilot needs to work

45 min
Old way
Assume everyone already has the latest apps.
→
New way
Confirm Microsoft 365 Apps 2511 or later, OneDrive signed in, new Outlook, and the supporting services (Loop, Whiteboard) are enabled for the pilot.

Why it matters

Copilot features depend on current clients and signed-in services. The Microsoft 365 Copilot app installs automatically on Windows devices running Apps 2511+; older channels and some regions don’t. Features like Planner Agent fail silently if Loop is off.

Where to turn it on · 10 min

Microsoft 365 Apps admin center → Customization → Device Configuration. Review the Copilot app rollout and your channel. In the Microsoft 365 admin center, enable Loop workspaces and Whiteboard for the pilot group.

✓ Done when

  • Pilot devices on Current or Monthly Enterprise Channel, 2511 or newer
  • Loop, Whiteboard and new Outlook on for the pilot

Switch 3 · Teams meetings

Set the Copilot meeting policy to “On with saved transcript required”

30 min
Old way
Copilot off, or Copilot on without a transcript, so the AI forgets the meeting the moment it ends.
→
New way
Meeting policy sets Copilot to On with saved transcript required. Transcript is on, retention runs, recap works after the fact.

Why it matters

This is the default Microsoft now ships and the only setting that reliably gives you recap, catch-up and interpreter after the meeting ends. Without a saved transcript Copilot answers while the meeting is live and then forgets.

Where to turn it on · 10 min

Teams admin center → Meetings → Meeting policies → open your Global policy → Recording & transcription → set Copilot to On with saved transcript required. Confirm Transcription is On.

✓ Done when

  • Policy saved on Global and the pilot group policy
  • Pilot test meeting shows the “Recap” tab populated

Switch 4 · Teams calls

Enable Copilot in Teams calls, with transcript

20 min
Old way
Copilot works in meetings but not in 1:1 or calling-plan calls, so sales and support staff miss the easiest wins.
→
New way
Calling policy sets Copilot to EnabledWithTranscript with AllowTranscriptionForCalling on, so Copilot catches up calls the same way it catches up meetings.

Why it matters

Teams calling and PSTN calls are where customer-facing teams spend half their day. Call recap after a customer call is often the “aha moment” that drives adoption.

Where to turn it on · 10 min

PowerShell: Set-CsTeamsCallingPolicy -Identity <pilot policy> -Copilot EnabledWithTranscript -AllowTranscriptionForCalling $true

✓ Done when

  • Pilot users see the Copilot icon during a 1:1 call
  • Interpreter available where languages matter

Switch 5 · SharePoint

Turn on Restricted Content Discovery for your high-risk sites

60 min
Old way
Enable Copilot, discover oversharing in the first week, panic.
→
New way
Before the pilot opens, use SharePoint Advanced Management to list the top overshared, ownerless and sensitive sites, and turn on Restricted Content Discovery (RCD) on each one.

Why it matters

Copilot shows people what they already have access to, so oversharing is the biggest Copilot risk, and it was there before Copilot arrived. RCD removes a site from Copilot and tenant-wide search without changing anyone’s permissions, buying time to fix it properly.

Where to turn it on · 10 min

SharePoint admin center → Reports → run the Content Management Assessment. Then Sites → Active sites → open a flagged site → Settings → turn Restrict content from Microsoft Copilot on. Repeat per site, or use Catalog management for a group.

✓ Done when

  • Top 10 flagged sites have RCD on, or an owner and a remediation plan
  • Report of RCD coverage filed with your security lead

Switch 6 · Purview

Set up Purview DLP for Copilot

45 min
Old way
Rely on access permissions alone, so one mislabelled file ends up in a Copilot answer.
→
New way
Purview DLP for Copilot excludes files with sensitivity labels like Confidential or Highly Confidential from Copilot grounding, and can block prompts containing sensitive data.

Why it matters

DLP is the second layer of defence after RCD, and it works at the label and keyword level across the whole tenant. It keeps the right content in Copilot and the sensitive content out, without you having to find every site.

Where to turn it on · 10 min

Microsoft Purview portal → Data Loss Prevention → Policies → new policy → location Microsoft 365 Copilot. Scope to the pilot group, exclude the most sensitive labels from grounding, test, then expand.

✓ Done when

  • At least one DLP for Copilot policy live in test mode
  • Audit logs confirm Copilot no longer surfaces restricted content

Switch 7 · Agents

Open the Agent 365 registry and set governance before people build

60 min
Old way
Agents appear in the Agent Store with no owners, no approval and no audit.
→
New way
Agent 365 registry is the source of truth. Every agent has an owner. Microsoft-built agents are approved per group. New agent requests go through a lightweight path. Blocking is in place.

Why it matters

Agents act under a real identity and reach your data. They need the same treatment as any user: ownership, approvals, and the ability to block fast. Agent 365 gives you one place to do it.

Where to turn it on · 15 min

Microsoft 365 admin center → Agents → All agents → Registry. Assign the AI Administrator role to one named person. Review each agent, assign owners, publish a request form in Viva Engage and run the Requests tab weekly.

✓ Done when

  • Every active agent has an owner
  • A written request and approval path (even one page) exists

Switch 8 · MCP

Govern Model Context Protocol servers in Copilot Studio

45 min
Old way
Any maker can wire any MCP server to an agent, so agents reach systems your security team never saw.
→
New way
Generative orchestration is on where needed, Power Platform DLP policies decide which connectors agents may use, and Agent 365 controls which MCP servers are available tenant-wide.

Why it matters

MCP connectors inherit your Power Platform data-loss-prevention policies. If DLP is right, MCP is governed. If DLP is loose, MCP is loose. Set it before makers find out.

Where to turn it on · 15 min

Power Platform admin center → Policies → Data policies. Create a Copilot DLP policy that assigns each business connector to Business, Non-business or Blocked. In Copilot Studio, enable Generative Orchestration only where you need MCP. Review the gallery of enabled MCP servers in Agent 365.

✓ Done when

  • A written list of allowed, non-business and blocked connectors
  • Agent 365 tool controls set for the pilot group

Switch 9 · Connectors

Roll out Copilot connectors (Graph connectors) in stages

60 min
Old way
Point Copilot at every system on day one and hope the answers get better.
→
New way
Start with one high-value connector (ServiceNow, Jira, Salesforce or a file share). Staged rollout to the pilot. Measure answer quality before adding the next.

Why it matters

Each connector widens the data surface Copilot reasons over. More connectors isn’t always better: duplicated or stale content hurts quality. Staged rollout keeps the signal clean and the cost predictable.

Where to turn it on · 15 min

Microsoft 365 admin center → Settings → Search & intelligence → Connectors. Create a connection, run staged rollout to the pilot group, and check indexed items in Content. Ask the pilot for feedback for two weeks before adding the next.

✓ Done when

  • One connector live, scoped to the pilot
  • A measured improvement, or a decision not to continue

Switch 10 · Cost

Set a Copilot Credit spending policy with budget and alerts

30 min
Old way
Pay-as-you-go for Cowork, agents and connectors with no limit, so finance finds out at the invoice.
→
New way
A spending policy per team with a monthly budget, pay-as-you-go on top of pre-purchased credits, and alerts at 50, 80 and 100 percent.

Why it matters

Agent 365, Cowork and MCP calls are usage-based. The three ways costs run away: wrong model for the task, agents left looping overnight, and no owner per service. Alerts give you the signal, policies give you the control.

Where to turn it on · 10 min

Microsoft 365 admin center → Copilot → Cost management. Create a spending policy for the pilot, link an Azure subscription for pay-as-you-go, set a monthly budget and alerts at 50, 80 and 100 percent, with named owners.

✓ Done when

  • Every paid service has a policy, a budget and an owner
  • Monthly cost review booked with finance for 30 days out
