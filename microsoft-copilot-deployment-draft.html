Microsoft 365 Copilot: the step-by-step setup guide

An old-school admin setup guide. Follow the steps in order. Each step tells you where to click in the Microsoft 365 admin center, what to turn on, and when you’re done.

Who runs this: AI Administrator (or Global Administrator) Time: ~1 day admin, 2–3 days end-to-end Pilot size: 50–200 users across 3+ functions

Step 0 · Understand your organisation first

Before you touch a setting, know your starting point. Spend one morning in these reports so you deploy on evidence, not assumption.

  • Microsoft 365 usage reports — admin.microsoft.com → Reports → Usage. Active users per app over 7–180 days. Shows where your people already work: that’s where Copilot wins first.
  • Microsoft Adoption Score — Reports → Adoption Score. Six categories totalling 600 points, including AI adoption, benchmarked against similar organisations. Weak categories flag where training needs focus.
  • Microsoft Secure Score — Microsoft Defender portal. Your security baseline. Clear the top five recommended actions before you widen access with Copilot.
  • SharePoint Advanced Management Content Management Assessment — SharePoint admin center → Reports. Overshared, ownerless, inactive sites. Keep the list open for Step 8.
  • Microsoft Purview DSPM for AI — Purview portal. Overshared sites with sensitive content, risky sharing links. Keep this list open too.
  • Collaboration patterns — in the usage reports, note the ratio of Teams messages to meeting recordings to documents shared. Chat-heavy organisations get the fastest win from Teams recap. Document-heavy organisations get the fastest win from Word, Excel and SharePoint agents.

Write down three numbers you want to move in 90 days (e.g. weekly active Copilot users, meeting recaps opened, documents chatted-with). You’ll need them for Step 10’s cost review.

Use the admin center guided setup as your backbone

Microsoft already ships two setup wizards that walk you through most of what follows. Have both open while you work through this guide.

  1. Sign in to admin.microsoft.com.
  2. In the left pane, select … Show all → Setup.
  3. On the Setup page, open the Guided setups tab.

Prepare the Microsoft 365 foundation

Foundations+

Where: Microsoft 365 admin center › Setup › Guided setups › Prepare your environment guide

  1. Confirm your custom domains are verified and your DNS is clean.
  2. Open the Microsoft 365 Apps setup guide. Confirm your deployment is on Current Channel or Monthly Enterprise Channel, version 2511 or later. 2511 is what auto-installs the Microsoft Copilot app on Windows.
  3. Open the OneDrive setup guide. Confirm Known Folder Move and automatic sign-in. OneDrive is a prerequisite for many Copilot features.
  4. In Settings → Org settings → Microsoft 365 Groups, confirm Loop workspaces are enabled. Planner Agent needs Loop.
  5. In Settings → Org settings → Microsoft Whiteboard, enable Whiteboard for your tenant.
  6. Give at least one named person the AI Administrator Entra role. Keep the Global Administrator role for break-glass only.

Why: Copilot features depend on current clients and signed-in services. One missing prerequisite breaks a dozen features silently.

Done when:

  • Pilot devices on Current / Monthly Enterprise Channel, 2511+
  • OneDrive signed in, Loop and Whiteboard on
  • AI Administrator role assigned

Assign Copilot licences to a focused pilot

Licensing

Where: Microsoft 365 admin center › Billing › Licenses

  1. Create a Microsoft Entra security group called Copilot-Pilot. Add 50–200 users across three or more functions — sales, operations, IT, HR, finance.
  2. In Billing → Licenses, open Microsoft 365 Copilot and assign the licence to the Copilot-Pilot group.
  3. Add two or three named champions per team. These are the people other users will ask when something’s odd.
  4. Agree three success measures with the sponsor: weekly active users, scenarios adopted (e.g. meeting recap, chat with doc), and one hard business number tied to a scenario.

Why: A broad switch-on hides your early failures and burns the first impression. A focused pilot gives you measurable usage per team and the pattern your training plan builds on.

Done when:

  • Pilot group has owners, champions, and a Teams channel
  • Three success measures agreed with the sponsor

Distribute and pin the Microsoft Copilot app

Distribution

Where: Microsoft 365 admin center › Copilot › Settings › Pin Microsoft Copilot to the Windows taskbar

  1. Confirm your Windows 11 devices run Apps 2511 or later. The Microsoft Copilot app auto-installs in the background — nothing to package in Intune for most tenants.
  2. In Copilot → Settings, open Pin Microsoft Copilot to the Windows taskbar. For the pilot, choose Pin for selected groups and pick Copilot-Pilot. (You can switch to Pin for all devices after the pilot.) This setting needs an Intune Administrator role.
  3. For users who need to install manually (Mac, personal Windows, EEA tenants), send the download page. iOS and Android users get the Microsoft 365 Copilot app from the store.
  4. Changes take up to 8 hours on Windows 11 and up to 48 hours on older builds.

Why: You want one obvious icon on every pilot user’s taskbar, consistent across devices. Discoverability is the fastest adoption win.

Done when:

  • Copilot app on the taskbar for your pilot group
  • Mobile users have the app installed and signed in

Pin Copilot Chat in Teams and Outlook

Distribution

Where: Microsoft 365 admin center › Copilot › Settings › Pin Microsoft Copilot Chat  ·  Teams admin center › Teams apps › Setup policies

  1. In Copilot → Settings, confirm Pin Copilot Chat in Microsoft 365 apps is set to the default (pinned). As of January 2026 this applies to Teams and Outlook navigation across the whole tenant.
  2. Open the Teams admin center. Go to Teams apps → Setup policies. Create a policy called Copilot-Pilot, turn User pinning on, add Copilot to Pinned apps and rank it near the top.
  3. In Group policy assignment, assign that policy to Copilot-Pilot.
  4. Teams app changes take a few hours to propagate.

Why: The Copilot Chat icon in Teams and Outlook is where most users land first. If it’s not pinned, they’ll forget it exists.

Done when:

  • Copilot Chat visible in Teams and Outlook for the pilot
  • Pinned order checked on a pilot user’s client

Deploy and pin the right agents so people find them

Agents

Where: Microsoft 365 admin center › Agents › All agents › Registry

  1. Review the Registry. Filter by Missing an owner and assign owners. Filter by Availability: All users to see what’s already open to everyone.
  2. For each agent you want in the pilot (start with Researcher and Analyst from Microsoft, plus one or two role-specific agents), open the detail pane and select Install. Choose Specific users or groups and pick Copilot-Pilot.
  3. From the All agents page, open the … menu and select Manage pinned agents. You have three admin slots per user. Pin one all-user agent (e.g. an IT help agent) and reserve two slots for role-specific agents (sales → Sales Coach, HR → HR Q&A).
  4. Set up the Requests tab. When users request a blocked agent, you’ll get a notification; review and approve weekly.
  5. Publish an internal page explaining User-pinned vs Admin-pinned vs Microsoft-pinned so pilot users understand the layout.

Why: If the right agent isn’t visible in the first click, users default to generic chat and miss the point. Admin-pinning is the single highest-leverage adoption switch.

Done when:

  • Every agent in the pilot has a named owner
  • Up to three pinned agents per user group, aligned to role
  • Request-approval path documented

Turn on Copilot in Teams meetings and calls

Teams

Where: Teams admin center › Meetings › Meeting policies  ·  Teams admin center › Voice › Calling policies

  1. Open your Global meeting policy (or a pilot policy). Under Recording & transcription, set Transcription to On.
  2. Set Copilot to On with saved transcript required — the only setting that reliably gives you recap, catch-up and interpreter after the meeting ends.
  3. Open the Calling policies. Set the pilot policy’s Copilot value, from PowerShell: Set-CsTeamsCallingPolicy -Identity <policy> -Copilot EnabledWithTranscript -AllowTranscriptionForCalling $true
  4. Test a 1:1 meeting and a VoIP call with a pilot user. Confirm the Recap tab is populated and the Copilot icon appears during the call.

Why: Without a saved transcript Copilot only answers while the meeting is live and then forgets. Calls are where customer-facing teams get the fastest wins.

Done when:

  • Meeting policy saved on Global or the pilot policy
  • Call policy on, test call shows the Copilot icon
  • Recap tab populated on a test meeting

Connect Copilot to your CRM, ERP and website

Connectors

Where: Microsoft 365 admin center › Settings › Search & intelligence › Data sources › Connectors

  1. Pick one high-value source first. Common starters:
    • CRM — Salesforce, Dynamics 365, HubSpot
    • ERP / line-of-business — SAP, Oracle, SQL databases, Dynamics 365 Finance
    • Service management — ServiceNow Tickets or Knowledge, Zendesk
    • Project & dev — Jira, Confluence, Azure DevOps
    • Website / intranet — Enterprise Websites (public and intranet URLs, up to 50 per connection)
    • HR / people — Workday, BambooHR
  2. In Connectors, select the prebuilt connector. Click Add, provide the credentials (service account recommended) and the connection name.
  3. Use the connector’s Enhance Copilot discovery page to write a good description and keywords. The description tells Copilot when to use the connector.
  4. Choose Staged rollout and scope to the Copilot-Pilot group. Index the content, then wait for the first full crawl (hours to days).
  5. For on-premises content, install the Microsoft Graph connector agent on a Windows server inside your network.
  6. After one week, review the Content tab. Ask pilot users for a Copilot answer that cites the new source. If quality is good, add the next connector. One at a time.

Why: Copilot is only as smart as the data it can reach. One well-indexed connector often beats three noisy ones — stale or duplicated content hurts answer quality.

Done when:

  • One connector live, scoped to the pilot
  • Pilot users can get Copilot to cite the new source
  • A written list of the next three connectors, prioritised

Lock down oversharing before people chat with the tenant

Security

Where: SharePoint admin center › Reports  ·  Microsoft Purview portal › Data Loss Prevention

  1. In the SharePoint admin center, run the Content Management Assessment. Export the list of overshared, ownerless and inactive sites you saw in Step 0.
  2. For each high-risk site, go to Active sites → open the site → Settings → turn on Restrict content from Microsoft Copilot. For many sites at once, use Catalog management.
  3. In the Purview portal, open Data Loss Prevention → Policies. Create a policy with location Microsoft 365 Copilot, scope to the pilot group, exclude files with your top sensitivity labels from grounding. Start in Test mode.
  4. After a week in test mode, review Purview audit logs. If no false positives, switch the policy to On.
  5. Document which sites are restricted and the owner remediation plan for each.

Why: Copilot shows people what they already have access to. Oversharing is the biggest Copilot risk, and it was there before Copilot arrived. RCD removes a site from Copilot discovery without changing anyone’s permissions, giving owners time to fix the real issue.

Done when:

  • Top 10 risky sites have RCD on or an owner and plan
  • A DLP for Copilot policy live in test or production

Set a Copilot Credit budget with alerts

Cost

Where: Microsoft 365 admin center › Copilot › Cost management

  1. In Cost management, create a spending policy for Copilot-Pilot. Link an Azure subscription for pay-as-you-go.
  2. Set a monthly budget. Add alert thresholds at 50, 80 and 100 percent, routed to a mail-enabled security group with IT and finance.
  3. Decide which services the policy covers: Cowork, SharePoint agents, Copilot Studio agents, pay-as-you-go. Assign a named owner per service.
  4. Remember: hitting 100 percent of the budget sends an alert — it does not stop billing. Only disconnecting a service stops the clock.

Why: Usage-based services bill per call. The three ways costs run away: wrong model for the task, agents left looping, and no owner per service.

Done when:

  • Every paid service has a policy, a budget and an owner
  • Monthly cost review booked with finance for 30 days out

Validate the pilot and hand over to users

Go-live

Where: Microsoft 365 admin center › Reports › Usage  ·  aka.ms/CopilotLicenseDetails

  1. Run the Copilot License Details diagnostic for three pilot users to confirm the licence, apps and policies are correct.
  2. Walk two pilot users through a scripted test: open the Copilot app, run a Teams meeting recap, chat with a document, call a pinned agent, ask a question that must come from your new connector. Fix anything that fails.
  3. Baseline the three measures from Step 2 in Reports → Usage and the Copilot Dashboard in Viva Insights.
  4. Send a short welcome message to the pilot: what’s pinned, where to ask for help, what to try first. Keep it under 150 words.
  5. Book 30-, 60- and 90-day reviews with the sponsor. Each one should answer: are the three numbers moving, what’s the next connector or agent, and should we expand the pilot?

Why: A validated pilot is the evidence you need to go wider. A pilot you can’t measure is a pilot you can’t defend.

Done when:

  • License diagnostic clean for three users
  • Scripted test passed end-to-end
  • 30/60/90-day reviews booked
